Four films: PLExyz Grid, Janka, AX Frame and AntaraX

Three short product promos and one longer AntaraX film of the products at work, with real people. Every scene is cast with actors at hired locations, and every screen shown is recorded from the products' demo workspaces. Claims follow the website: no figures we can't stand behind, and AI shown only where the product has it.

PLExyz Grid, 60s Janka, 60s AX Frame, 50s AntaraX film, 3 min Production notes

Production notes

Locations to hire

  • Warehouse with a yard and loading dock, Klang Valley. Racking, a dock door, room for a lorry to reverse. Grid, and the Grid chapter of the film.
  • Small distributor's store with shelving, a packing bench and a desk. Janka.
  • Open-plan office for a team of about 40. AX Frame.
  • Metal fabrication workshop with a laser cutter or bending machine and a small office. Yara, in the AntaraX film only.

Cast

  • All roles are actors. Every name in this storyboard is fictional.
  • Cast to look like the people who do these jobs in Malaysia: a mix of ages, Malay, Chinese and Indian Malaysians, men and women in working roles.
  • Work clothes, not costumes: hi-vis and safety boots in the warehouse, overalls in the workshop, smart-casual in the office.
  • Signed release forms for every person on camera, including background extras.

Screens to record

  • Record every product screen from its demo workspace at 2x resolution, then composite onto the device in the edit. Never film a live screen.
  • Demo data only. No real customer names, no real order values.
  • Grid: warehouse floor with zones, a booking with a placement warning then a fit, the unit search, zone utilisation.
  • Janka: reorder list, one item's forecast chart, a drafted purchase order with its reasoning, reading a delivery-order photo.
  • AX Frame: pasting a list, the subscription ledger with owners, a renewal warning, an Optimize proposal.
  • Yara: the worker's phone with Start, Hold and Complete, the work-order board updating.

Look and sound

  • Natural light, handheld but steady. The before scenes run slightly warmer and busier, the after scenes calmer and more open.
  • On-screen text in Poppins, white on a navy band, with the sparkle marker in the product's colour.
  • End cards carry the BaTech kain: a bed rising from the bottom edge, and the logo.
  • Music: light percussion with a Malaysian texture. The voiceover is calm and plain, never salesy.

Subtitles in three languages

  • Keep each on-screen line to about 40 characters in English. Bahasa Malaysia runs about a fifth longer, so leave room.
  • Japanese: at most about 16 characters per line, two lines at most, set in Noto Sans JP.
  • Product names stay in Latin letters in every language: PLExyz Grid, Janka, AX Frame, Yara, AntaraX.
  • No text baked into the footage itself (signs, labels) that the story depends on, so nothing needs re-shooting per language.

Before we film

  • AX Frame: how long before a renewal it warns, and by which channel. The film says "before the charge" until this is decided.
  • PLExyz Grid's AI is on the Intelligent tier and still being prepared for launch, so the Grid promo shows the rules check, not AI suggestions.
  • Yara's AI intake is planned, so the film shows Yara as it works today.
  • Calls to action: the page each film points to, and whether Frame says "Request pilot access".
